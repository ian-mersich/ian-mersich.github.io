Superbench · Learning guide

Explore real datasets

Bring measurements into one project while keeping their source, observation identities and meaning clear.

This guide covers the Superbench desktop workspace, which is in development. A public desktop installer is not yet available. In Superbench, open Help & dataset examples from its menu to follow these guides beside your project.

Start here

Choose an import method

Use the route that fits your file and task. Importing a table does not normalize values or run a statistical test.

  1. Local files or copied tables

    Open CSV/TSV file or text, inspect the preview, and select the observation ID column. Keep identifiers such as 001 as text. For a feature-by-row matrix, explicitly choose the features and observations to bring into an editable worksheet.

  2. A public table link

    Use a direct public HTTPS CSV/TSV link. Review each returned table before creating a worksheet. This route accepts up to eight files of 2 MiB each; sign-in pages, archives and URLs with query parameters need a different route.

  3. A complete downloaded study

    Use the dataset importer for supported cBioPortal ZIP/TAR/TAR.GZ studies or DepMap downloads with their metadata. Then use Imported datasets to select an assay and exact observations. Large source matrices stay outside the project; selected measurements can be captured into it.

  4. R or Python

    Inspect a larger assay in Code, then explicitly send a supported table to Worksheet and Explore. Preparing a script never runs it. Keep the original file, selected inputs and package versions with your work.

What to keep in mind
  • An editable project table must fit all limits: 5,000 rows, 200 columns, 200,000 cells and 2 MiB. Reviewing a larger source does not mean its complete matrix is embedded in the project.
  • Keep gene expression, dependency, drug sensitivity and clinical measurements as separate assays with their own units and scale.

Cancer cell lines

DepMap

Explore model-level measurements with exact ModelID or DepMap_ID identities and the matching model annotation file.

  1. Choose a release and assay

    Download the desired release from DepMap and retain its source information. The worked expression route uses the 24Q4 protein-coding stranded log2(TPM + 1) matrix and its matching Model.csv. That does not qualify every DepMap file.

  2. Review the matrix and model annotations

    Import the complete supported download through the dataset importer, or review a bounded local CSV selection. Join Model.csv by exact ModelID; do not match rows by their position or cell-line display name.

  3. Set up Explore

    Choose the expression assay, features and exact model cohort. Review the expression scale and model/tissue annotations before capture. A zero expression value is different from a missing measurement.

  4. Continue into an analysis

    Inspect matched expression measurements, create a descriptive graph or run the supported Pearson/Spearman comparison. Send the captured table to R/Python for another declared method. Expression association is not evidence of gene dependency or drug response.

What to keep in mind
  • Use provider downloads and your permitted local files. This guide does not enable automated DepMap retrieval or redistribute provider matrices.
  • A saved captured selection keeps its values. Browsing the complete source after moving computers requires that source to be available again.

Patient studies

cBioPortal

Keep study, molecular profile, sample and patient identities separate when exploring clinical and molecular measurements.

  1. Discover the study

    Search public study metadata and inspect the available assays. The current provider search retrieves descriptions, not clinical or molecular measurement rows. Download the study from its provider to continue.

  2. Import a study or a selected table

    Import a supported ZIP, TAR, TAR.GZ or TGZ study without manually extracting it, or review a downloaded text table. cBioPortal clinical tables have four descriptive comment records before their column header.

  3. Review samples and patients

    Use SAMPLE_ID for a sample-level assay and retain its PATIENT_ID mapping. Multiple samples from one patient are not independent patients. Keep patient-level clinical measurements separate from sample-level molecular values.

  4. Capture, compare and reopen

    The worked TCGA AML route uses the laml_tcga_pub RNA Seq V2 RSEM expression profile. Select exact samples and genes, review the matching and measurement meaning, create a graph, and reopen the saved project. Keep RSEM expression distinct from raw RNA counts, z-scores and copy number.

What to keep in mind
  • Study-level permissions and data-use terms still apply. Metadata discovery is not a claim that every molecular profile can be fetched automatically.
  • A shared gene name does not make assays, patients or cohorts interchangeable.

Single-cell and single-nucleus studies

CELLxGENE

Inspect a downloaded H5AD in Python, preserve cell and donor identities, and send a bounded table into the same Worksheet and Explore workspace.

  1. Choose the dataset and version

    Retain the dataset ID, collection, organism, assay, version, source citation and downloaded H5AD. The worked example is the Developmental Zebrafish Reference Atlas pancreas subset: 1,387 nuclei from Danio rerio, not a human cell-line cohort.

  2. Inspect locally in Code

    In the app Help, choose Prepare local H5AD inspection and enter the full local H5AD path. Inspect obs, var, embeddings and the declared expression layer before running a method. The script needs anndata and pandas; it does not install packages or download data.

  3. Publish a table deliberately

    The script creates cell_metadata with exact cell_id, donor_id and available annotations, plus source UMAP coordinates when present. It also creates donor_cell_counts as a descriptive count by donor and cell type. Explicitly send the chosen table from Python to Worksheet, then open Explore.

  4. Review the observation unit

    For cell_metadata choose Single cell / nucleus and retain donor_id as the biological-unit column. For donor_cell_counts retain both donor and cell type. Counts of observed cells describe the selected dataset; they are not tissue proportions or independent biological replicates. Use a donor-aware method for biological inference.

What to keep in mind
  • The H5AD remains an external source. A .freebench project does not automatically bundle it. The editable-table route here does not attach an entire H5AD as a managed Explore assay.
  • A provider UMAP is a source embedding, not a newly computed result. Do not interpret its coordinate correlation as an expression analysis. No cross-species matching, normalization, differential expression or cell-type annotation is performed by this inspection script.

Keep the experiment connected

Analyze and reopen

Retain the measurement definition and complete selected data as you move from exploration to an analysis, a graph and a saved project.

  1. Review the comparison

    Choose exact observation matching and inspect matched, missing and excluded records. Do not silently pair different patients, models or cells, infer transformations from a filename, or treat repeated observations as independent replicates.

  2. Choose the method

    Explore supports descriptive distributions and matched Pearson/Spearman comparisons. Worksheet and Analyze provide their supported scientific analyses and graphs; Code allows an explicit R/Python method. Count-expression analysis needs a declared complete raw-count assay and a reviewed design, not a selected log-expression table.

  3. Keep earlier Results

    Changing an analysis setup or its reviewed data creates a new Result. Inspect the earlier Result, its original inputs and the graph's source link rather than replacing its historical settings with the latest ones.

  4. Save, close and open again

    Save the whole project, reopen it in a fresh session and inspect the captured values, scripts, source definitions, Gene Contexts and Results. Reopening does not run code, download data or restore packages automatically. Keep external source files and use their explicit recovery controls when needed.

What to keep in mind
  • These examples demonstrate supported workflows; they do not establish biological findings, all-provider coverage or complete third-party format parity.
