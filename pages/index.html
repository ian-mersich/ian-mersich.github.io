About me

I’m a translational biologist combining computational analysis with molecular and cellular experiments to investigate how proteostasis, RNA regulation, and adaptive cell states shape disease and therapeutic response. I integrate molecular profiles with chemical and genetic perturbations to identify candidate mechanisms and design experiments to test them.

I also develop predictive models and scientific tools for exploring biological data, generating hypotheses, and preparing figures. My work connects experimental pharmacology, computational biology, and tool development.

I earned my PhD in Cancer and Cell Biology at the University of Cincinnati, studying metabolism and therapeutic vulnerabilities in pediatric brain tumors, and completed graduate training in integrative genomics. Before my PhD, I spent about six years in pharmaceutical development at Boehringer Ingelheim, Gilead Sciences, and CustoPharm, working on analytical methods, therapeutic antibodies, and quality control.

Research

I use integrative computational analyses across diverse biological data to identify candidate mechanisms and design experiments to test them. My current research is focused on the HSP90 chaperone network and proteostasis across normal, developmental, and disease states, with an emphasis on pediatric neuro-oncology and neurological disorders.
